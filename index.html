How it works

What it learns

A map of your work

Sparrow reads what you let it see: meetings, your screen, the tools you already use. From that it keeps a map of your work and who is in it. The map stays on your laptop.

What it decides

Who needs to know

It works out when someone else needs to know something, who that is, and exactly what they need in order to do their part.

What it hands off

Bigger jobs, minus the sensitive parts

For a task that needs a bigger model, Sparrow sends just that task, with names, numbers, and recordings removed first. The model does the work. Your Sparrow keeps the picture.

Up and down the org

A nervous system for the company.

Most of a manager's week is relaying: direction downward, status upward, blockers sideways. Sparrow does the relaying, so the people can do the managing.

Everyone

Where it is going: machines too

Your systems can report in too.

Your GPU queue, your CI, your nightly jobs. We are building Sparrow to read them the way it reads a meeting, through an integration, an API, or the screen itself, so that when a job fails at 2 a.m., only the one person who can fix it hears about it. Design partners with a shared cluster will shape this first.

Why on-device

Let a local AI know everything about your work. Let no company know it.

Sparrow learns from the raw record on your laptop, so it can know more about you than any cloud assistant safely could. When it needs outside help, it sends one redacted slice: no names, no numbers, no recordings, just the context the question needs.

Nothing to breach

Transcripts, screens, and notes stay on the laptop they came from. There is no central copy to leak, subpoena, or sell. There is no copy for us to read, and none for your employer either.

Built to run small

A model distilled for one job, understanding one person's work, costs a fraction of a frontier model per token. We are training that model now. Today the reasoning runs through a frontier model, sent only a redacted slice of the record, under a cap you set. The recordings never go.

A person approves every action

Sending, buying, or changing anything stops for a person to approve, and what you approve is exactly what runs. Something the model read or heard can inform a draft, and that is as far as it goes.

Delete it all, and get a receipt

Stop capture in one click. Delete everything in one more, and get a receipt naming every directory that was emptied. A deleted memory is gone everywhere it ever existed, because it only ever existed in one place.

Design partners

Where this is

A working prototype, shaped by the teams who use it first.

Sparrow runs today on our own machines. So far that's the only place it has run, which is what the design partner program is for. Windows first, macOS next.

Working today

Capture from meetings and the screen. A searchable memory where every fact links to the moment it came from. A graph of people, commitments, and open loops. Two assistants paired so one can ask the other, under each person's disclosure rules. Actions in the browser and on the desktop that stop for approval. One-click stop, export, and delete with a receipt.

Being built now

The distilled model that moves the reasoning onto the device. Integrations with the machines a team depends on. The flow of priorities down and status up across an organization. These are the decisions design partners get a say in.
